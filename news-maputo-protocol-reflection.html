Women's Rights · September 2026

Rights on Paper Must Become Rights in Practice

Lessons from our Maputo Protocol work in Wau, shared at a regional panel on feminist organizing.

At a regional panel on feminist organizing amid anti-gender and anti-rights backlash, WDG shared lessons from a sub-grant implemented in Wau with support from the SIHA Fund (KOMBOA).

An important opening

South Sudan's ratification of the Maputo Protocol has given women's rights organizations a stronger basis to raise awareness and to engage government institutions, communities and justice actors on their responsibilities. Women in our sessions welcomed the Protocol because it speaks to issues that shape their daily lives.

Laws on paper, barriers in practice

Women continue to face barriers around marriage, divorce, inheritance, child custody, children's nationality, family planning and marriage certification. These are driven by limited knowledge, poor access to procedures and restrictive social norms. Economic hardship came up again and again as a major cause of marital breakdown, which is why rights work and economic empowerment have to go hand in hand.

From training to collective action

Women and activists are already organizing:

  • A committee has formed to follow up on marriage certification.
  • Women are calling for refresher training on the Maputo Protocol and rights laws.
  • Radio talk shows have been proposed to reach more people.
  • Simplified, multilingual information materials are being developed.
  • Groups are engaging the judiciary and law-enforcement institutions.
  • Women's groups are folding rights awareness into their own plans.
"The changing context for feminist organizing is not only about whether laws exist, but whether women can actually understand, claim, and enjoy the rights those laws provide. Awareness must lead to organized action, institutional accountability, and sustained advocacy." — WDG & partners, regional panel statement
